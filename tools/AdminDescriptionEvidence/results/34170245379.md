## Decision

The predeclared limits remain 3% for completed throughput and CPU/call, 5% for actual call p50/p99/max, and one byte/call allocation allowance, against each baseline and for control drift. No bounds were widened.

- Legacy probe throughput is 5.88%/9.91% lower and CPU/call 6.24%/10.99% higher than A1/A2. Throughput control drift is 4.47%, exceeding 3%.
- Delete probe throughput is 5.03%/6.22% lower, CPU/call 5.30%/6.63% higher, p50 6.17%/7.27% higher and maximum 14.53%/14.46% higher. Its probe controls are within the declared drift bounds. These losses remain an unresolved product/process concern.
- Inventory throughput drift is 4.82% and p99 drift is -15.32%; candidate CPU and p99 exceed A2 limits. Legacy maximum drift is -24.37%.
- Every BDN configuration has JIT activity in intervals wholly inside the actual-workload boundaries: 7–31 methods, roughly 5.7–33.6 ms in those interior intervals. Boundary-overlap totals also include adjacent-stage compilation and must not all be attributed to workload. Method-level attribution is missing.
- Separate probes record measured JIT in inventory A1 and four new-capability cases; mixed/malformed also retain final-warmup JIT, and several new cases grow thread-pool threads during measurement. The retry probe is JIT-quiet but does not validate other workloads or the BDN engine.
- BDN and probe point estimates differ: legacy BDN mean improves versus A1 while the probe loses throughput; delete BDN A1/A2 drift is about -7.02%. This evidence does not isolate a confirmed product effect or establish protected-metric equivalence.
- Therefore the full experiment is INCONCLUSIVE. All losses and samples remain visible. No identical repeat is funded: first isolate the actual-stage transitions with method-level evidence and investigate legacy/delete process differences. A longer GlobalSetup alone has not primed the later BDN engine path. This last explanation is a hypothesis, not an attribution.
