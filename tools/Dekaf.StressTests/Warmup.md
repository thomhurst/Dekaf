# Producer warmup and measurement boundaries

Duration-based producer scenarios use `--producer-warmup-seconds` (default 180, minimum 20). A local diagnostic with 60 seconds still observed compilation during measurement, so the default allows longer continuous load in each reuse cycle. This remains a starting policy, not proof that JIT has stopped. Declare the same duration for every A1/B/A2 process before a comparison. Warmup does not extend individual phases until they look good. If the retained runtime evidence shows startup activity, the experiment is inconclusive; investigate it and predeclare a revised campaign.

`ProducerWorkload` runs the same producer loop, delivery-latency sampling, resource reporting, allocation sampler and runtime observers in warmup and measurement. The producer stays alive across six load/drain cycles and measurement. Payload size, rotating key distribution, compression, acknowledgements, batching, connection policy and synchronous-versus-awaited delivery behavior match the measured workload. Drains exercise object return and reuse at the workload's actual concurrency. This primes the working pool demand; it does not prove that every internal pool is at its maximum capacity. Artificially filling every pool would create a different workload.

Warmup traffic and counters are separate from measurement. Each cycle must run its full declared load duration and complete without reported errors. Drain time is recorded separately from active workload time. The broker watermark check establishes the post-warmup starting offset after all warmup traffic drains. No forced collection follows warmup, and histogram pages are touched before loading the producer. Sampled delivery callbacks finish before measurement stops; their tasks are not retained in a growing collection. The duration timer stops ingress, while runtime sampling continues through final delivery drain.

Results retain process-wide `JitInfo` compilation count/time, thread-pool size/backlog, CPU time, allocated bytes, GC counts/pause duration, managed heap and working-set observations. These include harness work; they are not isolated client CPU or allocation measurements. See the [.NET runtime metric definitions](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dotnet/core/diagnostics/built-in-metrics-runtime). The warmup/runtime validator requires complete coverage, a final ten seconds of active warmup without JIT or thread-pool size changes, and no such transitions inside measurement. Setup between warmup and measurement is retained but is not misclassified as measured compilation. Missing coverage, startup transitions or mismatched warmup durations yield `INCONCLUSIVE`; samples and maxima are never trimmed. A quiet counter series is a necessary check, not proof of statistical equivalence or long-run stability. Traces are needed to attribute observed compilation to specific methods.

The exact-SHA stress workflow uses one `ubuntu-latest` VM, with brokers on CPUs 0–1 and the client on CPUs 2–3. Both products compile the candidate harness and build configuration. A fresh-main ancestry check, build-server shutdown, and separate one-minute correctness runs for both fixtures precede A1/B/A2. Every process creates fresh Testcontainers brokers, including single-broker lanes. Provenance records product/harness SHAs, runtime and runner settings, fixture adaptation, binary hashes and main at the end. The paid scheduled lanes retain their runner selection and coverage.

This warmup applies to duration-based producer scenarios. Consumer, transactional, round-trip and BenchmarkDotNet workloads require their own representative priming and startup evidence. In particular, BenchmarkDotNet `GlobalSetup` loops do not prove that its generated engine and actual-stage observers are warm. These checks do not replace the protected-metric comparison, hot-path MemoryDiagnoser evidence, or longer stability/recovery/shutdown validation required by `AGENTS.md`.
